What’s new in RapidReconciler.

Newest changes appear at the top — each entry is auto-published from a merge to main. Spot something missing or want context on a fix? rrsupport@getgsi.com.

August 13, 2026

Reopening a reviewed period is now possible when work has already left it. Reopening asks for a reason and records who reversed it before anything changes, and marking a period reviewed now appears in the audit findings alongside your card findings. The Audit tab is now titled Reconciliation Audit Findings. Clearer wording on how the roll-forward figure relates to the reconciling items, and the Export Analyzer now tells you why an analysis could not be produced instead of reporting a timeout.

August 13, 2026

The daily summary now starts with the period you are actually looking at, and names another period explicitly when something there needs attention first. On the transaction variance chart, the roll-forward figure and the reconciling-items figure now explain their difference whenever they appear to disagree.

August 12, 2026

Excel export on a Transaction Details row could produce no file and no message. Both are fixed: the export completes, and if anything does go wrong the page now says what and what to do about it. Sign-in errors are clearer, and no longer suggest signing in again when that is not the problem. Variance card findings are shorter and lead with what happened and what to do.

August 12, 2026

Excel exports now explain themselves when they fail instead of doing nothing, and error messages tell you whether to sign in again, contact IT, or narrow your filter. Status colours across the admin pages are driven by the condition rather than by the colour name, and the figures on Analyst Home now say which period and which totals they cover.

* fix(helpdesk): 5,615 error lines were three problems, and the analyzer showed six identical headings

A real customer log: 128,216 lines, 5,615 containing ERROR, spanning 17 hours. They collapse to THREE conditions. The analyzer detected them correctly and then presented six critical rows whose headings were visually identical, because the 100-character truncation cut off before the message — every heading was timestamp, PID, thread and logger boilerplate — and the dedupe key could not collapse Java stacks (1,805 occurrences split four ways on a lambda ordinal).

Now: one row per condition, each with a count, a time span, and a fix. Counts are EVENTS, not lines — the read-only failure emits two log lines per event. The verdict leads with how many distinct problems need action. A rule that fires on 17,888 entries no longer emits 17,888 DOM nodes into a collapsed disclosure nobody opens; 400, with counts still exact.

The root cause is confirmed in source, not inferred. A null `databaseJobName` reaches `SynchronizeService:132`, which dereferences it — and only on the branch taken when nothing changed, which is why it fires steadily on an idle instance rather than at startup. The same null is concatenated into SQL at `DatabaseManagerServiceMsSqlImpl:294`, writing the literal string 'null' behind a guard a Java null can never satisfy, so it retries forever. Harmless on a writable database; on a read-only one it throws every tick.

Also: AN-13's residual is shaped and the verdict is that nothing separates it — 577 of 589 rows across all three demos are IM and fail Mfg Cost Mismatch at a DocType='IC' test. Recorded rather than papered over with a new card.

Registries updated: shared-state-registry records why the admin surface keeps a severity ladder distinct from the analyzer's result vocabulary; shared-figure- registry records the viewed / open-window / data-current distinction.

Release-Note: The log analyzer now reports one row per problem with a count, a time span and a fix, instead of repeating the same error hundreds of times. Large log files load much faster.

August 10, 2026

Abbreviated money figures now round consistently across the Home page, so a summary chip and the detail below it can no longer differ by a rounding step. The Data Health band now names which GL classes are excluded rather than only counting them, and the model routing list states that an excluded class is one with no routing in the model.

August 10, 2026

Every column on the Transaction Details grid and the Variance Analyzer table now explains itself on hover, including what a zero in the Ledger column does and does not mean and which key the general ledger is matched on for each transaction type.

August 10, 2026

Ask the analyst assistant about WIP revaluation and it now names the programs involved, R30837 driven by R30822, instead of declining to. It still points you at the version in use, since the processing options for those programs differ site to site.

August 10, 2026

A period marked reviewed by mistake can now be reopened from the Transaction Variance panel, as long as nothing has left that period yet: no cards handed off to the Audit Center and no corrective adjustments recorded. Once work has moved on, the panel explains that reopening needs to be recorded against whoever does it rather than undone quietly.

August 10, 2026

The Sales AAI Cancels card is now called Sales DMAAI Net Zero, which names what it actually finds: the two cost-of-sales account instructions for an order type resolving to one account, so the debit and credit net to zero and no journal entry is written. Card guidance no longer quotes figures from sample data. On the Transaction Variance cards, the review chip now reads "Let ride" instead of "Reviewed", because it records that the period was signed off with the card untouched rather than that the card itself was reviewed.

August 10, 2026

The Period Mismatch card now reports how far apart the two months actually are, so a genuine month-end cut-off is not presented the same way as a document offsetting nine months later. The Unclassified Inventory card no longer implies both sides of every transaction posted, because on most of those rows only one side did.
