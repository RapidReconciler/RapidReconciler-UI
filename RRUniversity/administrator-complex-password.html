RapidReconciler supports an optional complex password policy designed to protect sensitive financial reconciliation data and align with common enterprise security standards. You choose which companies require it — turning it on for the companies that need it without forcing it on the rest. This guide covers what each setting requires, how to choose which companies use it, the reset process, and administrative controls.

Overview

Every RapidReconciler password meets a standard baseline. On top of that, your RapidReconciler administrator can require complex passwords for the companies that need stronger protection — set on the Complex Passwords page, one company at a time or all at once.

§Standard vs. complex

Standard — always onComplex — when a company requires it
At least 8 characters. Everything in Standard, plus the rules below.
Can’t reuse your current password. Characters from at least 3 of 4 groups — uppercase, lowercase, digit, special.
No run of 3+ characters from your name or email.
Can’t match any of your last 10 passwords.
i

Why per company? A password belongs to a person, but a company is an access scope. Because one person can work in more than one company, the strongest rule wins: if someone can reach any company that requires a complex password, they’ll be asked for one. Turning it on for a single company effectively covers everyone who can reach that company.

People who sign in with single sign-on (SSO) are never affected by these rules — their identity provider handles password strength.

One exception. An administrator can switch single sign-on off for one person: a service account, someone not yet in your identity provider, or break-glass access during an outage. That person signs in with a RapidReconciler password instead, so these rules do apply to them, and so does the 90-day password expiry. You can see who is in that state on the RR Team page: their sign-in reads Password with a bypasses marker beside it.

Password Requirements

When at least one company a person can reach requires complex passwords, every new password that person sets must meet the following criteria:

RequirementDetail
Minimum length8 characters (this applies to every password, complex or not)
Not the current passwordCannot be the same as the password it replaces (also applies to every password)
Character complexityCharacters from at least 3 of the 4 groups below
Name restrictionNo run of 3 or more consecutive characters from the person’s name or email address
Password historyCannot match any of the last 10 passwords

Separately from this setting, every RapidReconciler password expires 90 days after it was last set, and the person is asked to choose a new one at their next sign-in. People who sign in through your identity provider have no RapidReconciler password, so nothing expires for them.

§Character Complexity

Your password must include characters from any three of the four categories listed below — you do not need to use all four:

  • English uppercase characters (A through Z)
  • English lowercase characters (a through z)
  • Base 10 digits (0 through 9)
  • Special characters: anything that isn’t one of the above (e.g. !, $, #, %)

Logging In

The sign-in screen (Welcome back) asks for Email and Password, with a Forgot your password? link under the password field and a Sign in button. If your email domain uses single sign-on, the password field and the link are replaced by a button that continues to your identity provider.

A person who signs in with a password is taken to the Set a new password screen in two cases:

  1. Their password is more than 90 days old, or was never set. The screen says Your password has expired or needs to be reset. Choose a new one below.
  2. They clicked Forgot your password?, entered their address on the Password reset screen, clicked Reset, and then followed the link sent to their email. The screen says Choose a new password for your account.
i

Note: The password reset link sent by email is time-limited. If the link has expired, the user should repeat the "Forgot your password?" process to receive a new link. If the issue persists, contact your RapidReconciler administrator.

Resetting a Password

The Set a new password screen lists the rules under Your new password must meet:, then asks for New password and Confirm new password. Click Update password to save. The new password must conform to the policy outlined above.

When a password has expired, the screen also offers Forgot your current password? Reset it by email, for someone who doesn’t remember the old one.

Common reasons a new password may be rejected:

  • The password is fewer than 8 characters
  • The password is the same as the current one
  • The password does not meet the three-category complexity requirement
  • The password contains 3 or more consecutive characters from the user’s name or email address
  • The password matches one of the last 10 previously used passwords

The two fields must match: if they don’t, the screen says New password and confirmation do not match. When a rule is violated, a list below the Update password button names each rule the password failed:

If your password is being rejected and you are unsure why, review the requirements above and try a different combination of characters.

Administration

§How New Users Get a Password

When you add a team member who signs in with a password, RapidReconciler emails them a secure, single-use link to set their own. Whatever password they choose must meet the complex rules if any company they can reach requires them. Someone on a single sign-on domain gets no link: they sign in through your identity provider.

You can also set a password for someone yourself, in the Password field of the Edit team member dialog on the RR Team page (at least 8 characters).

For step-by-step instructions on adding users, see Manage the Team
!

Best Practice: The set-password link is time-limited, and the RR Team page has no control to re-send it. If a new hire doesn’t set their password before the link expires, they can click Forgot your password? on the sign-in page to get a fresh link sent to the same address.

§Turning Complex Passwords On or Off

You manage this yourself on the Complex Passwords page. On Home, find the Team & Access card and click its third row, marked with a padlock. The row reads Complex passwords, or the state of your password-policy review (for example Review password policy). The page lists the companies licensed on the database you’re signed in to, with an on/off switch for each. Turn it on for a single company, or use Require for all to switch every company at once. The rules apply the next time an affected person sets a password. See Choosing which companies require it below.

!

Recommendation: Keep complex passwords required for any company that holds production financial data. A training or sandbox company can stay on the standard rules.

§Locked or Inaccessible Accounts

If a user is unable to log in and cannot complete the password reset process (for example, they no longer have access to the email address on file), open them on the RR Team page with the pencil icon:

  • If the email address is wrong, correct Email and click Save Changes. They can then use Forgot your password? on the sign-in page.
  • Or type a new password in Password, save, and pass it to them.

Someone who signs in through your identity provider has no RapidReconciler password: their access problem belongs with your identity provider.

See Manage the Team for instructions on editing a user

Choosing Which Companies Require It

Open Complex Passwords from the third row of the Team & Access card on Home. Only administrators can open it. The page has three parts.

§Review reminder

A band at the top reminds you to review which companies require complex passwords. Click a button next to Remind me in:

  • 3 months or 6 months marks the setup as reviewed and sets the next reminder. The band then reads Reviewed with the reminder date.
  • Never turns the reminders off. The band then says periodic review reminders are off, and you can set one anytime.

The complex-passwords row on Home’s Team & Access card shows the same state.

§What each setting requires

The What each setting requires card shows the Standard rules (always on) beside the Complex rules (when a company requires it). They are the rules in Standard vs. complex above.

§Companies

The Companies card lists every company licensed on this database, with a count such as 2 of 5 require complex in its title. Its columns are Number, Company and Complex password. A company whose name can’t be read shows as Company and its number.

  • For one company, flip its switch. The switch reads Complex when it is on and Standard when it is off. Anyone who can reach that company must meet the complex rules the next time they set a password.
  • For all companies at once, use Require for all to turn it on everywhere, or Turn off for all to drop back to the standard rules. Each button is disabled when there is nothing left for it to change.

Each change saves straight away and a message at the bottom of the page confirms it. Existing users aren’t locked out when you turn it on: they keep their current password until it expires or they reset it, at which point the new rules apply.

i

Note: It’s a good idea to let people know in advance. The next password they set for an affected company will need to meet the complex rules.
