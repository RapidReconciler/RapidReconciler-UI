Account Roll Forward Walkthrough

Each inventory GL account should roll forward cleanly from one period to the next. This is the how-to for reading the Account Roll Forward page and squaring an account that doesn’t tie.

Module: Inventory You’ll need: RapidReconciler login · JD Edwards access for the repost Press Ctrl+F (or ⌘+F) to search this page

What the roll forward checks

The Account Roll Forward walks each inventory GL account period by period and confirms two chains tie:

  • The GL rolls forward. Beginning balance plus the period’s activity equals the ending balance, and each period’s ending balance carries into the next as its beginning balance.
  • The reconciliation variance rolls forward. The net variance the prior period left behind is the variance the current period starts from.

A break is a period where one of those chains doesn’t tie. The page’s AI brief reads this company’s roll forward for you — it names the account that’s off and the next step to take, in order. This page is the how behind that step.

When an account doesn’t roll forward (GL break)

A GL break means the Account Balances (F0902) don’t tie to the posted Account Ledger (F0911) for that account — the summarized balances and the posted detail disagree on what the period should be. Work it in this order.

  1. Repost first, in JD Edwards. Run Repost Account Balances (R099102) scoped to the company, account, and fiscal year — proof mode first to confirm what it will touch, then final mode. The repost rebuilds F0902 from the posted detail in F0911, which proves JD Edwards is internally consistent. Involve your JD Edwards administrator before you run it in final mode.
  2. Reload the GL, here. Once the repost is done, click Reload GL on the Account Roll Forward page, then Go in the dialog. This clears RapidReconciler’s own copy of the general ledger from the earliest out-of-balance period and re-pulls the corrected figures from JD Edwards on the next scheduled refresh. Your JD Edwards data is never changed.
    ✓
    What Reload GL does — and doesn’t

    It never changes JD Edwards data. It only clears and re-pulls RapidReconciler’s own copy of the general ledger, and it re-pulls on the normal refresh schedule — not on the spot.

  3. Wait for the next refresh, then confirm. The reload doesn’t re-pull immediately. It happens on the next scheduled load. After that load runs, re-open the Account Roll Forward and check the account’s GL OK column in the grid. It should read yes, or end · yes on the current open period.
i
Why this order

The repost proves JD Edwards right first. After that, any break left on the RapidReconciler side is only a gap between the two copies — RapidReconciler is missing rows JD Edwards has, or holding rows it doesn’t — and the reload corrects it in either direction in a single pass. Reload before you repost and you’d just pull the same out-of-balance figures back in.

The variance usually clears with it

A GL break feeds the reconciliation variance, so fixing the GL and letting the next refresh run typically squares the variance on the same account at the same time. The page has two buttons, Reload GL and Rebuild roll-forward, and both work from the GL. There’s no separate control for the variance.

A variance that’s off on its own — with no GL break behind it — needs no manual step. RapidReconciler recomputes the full period timeline on every refresh, so a variance that failed to roll forward in an earlier period is recomputed from the current data on the next run and ties again once that refresh completes.

If a break survives

After a repost, a reload, and a refresh, a break shouldn’t survive. If the corrected figures have loaded and the grid still shows the break, click Rebuild roll-forward. It re-merges the ledger RapidReconciler already holds and recomputes the report straight away, so you don’t wait for another refresh. It pulls nothing new from JD Edwards, so on a ledger that hasn’t been corrected it rebuilds to the same result.

If the break is still there after the rebuild finishes, it points at a data-integrity condition beyond these steps. Contact your IT department to investigate before taking any other action.

i

Reload Cardex is not a roll-forward fix. It’s a separate cardex data-integrity utility — don’t reach for it to clear a break that survives a refresh. When a break persists, the next step is an IT-department investigation, not another reload.

The page, section by section

The page shows one company, named under the title. It has no company switcher: it opens for the company you’re working on in Home, so pick a different company there and open the page again.

The RapidReconciler AI brief

The RapidReconciler AI card at the top reads this company’s roll forward. When something is off, it names what, the accounts involved and the next step in order. When nothing is off, it says so. Under the answer, What was sent shows exactly what went to the AI.

If the AI isn’t part of your plan, or is turned off for this database, the card says that instead. If the AI call fails, the card stays empty. It has no fallback text.

Band 1: Reload GL

Click a band’s heading to collapse or expand it. The text beside the heading sums up the GL side of the roll forward:

  • 2 GL accounts out of balance (the number varies): the account chips below the description name each account that doesn’t tie.
  • Reload requested — re-pulls on the next scheduled refresh: you clicked Go on a reload while the page was open, and the break is still in the loaded data. The page only remembers the request while it stays open, so if you come back before the refresh, the band shows the accounts as out of balance again.
  • No GL break found, but 3 accounts could not be evaluated: nothing compared is out of balance, but the chips name accounts with no prior period to roll forward from. Treat this as unproven, not clean.
  • GL rolled forward cleanly — nothing to reload: every account ties.
  • No roll-forward rows for this company — nothing checked: RapidReconciler has no rows to judge. This isn’t a clean result either.
  • Company scope not recognized — nothing checked: the link asked for a company outside your access, so the page shows nothing.

Reload GL opens a dialog that first checks what would reload. If the GL already rolls forward, it says there’s nothing to reload and Go stays greyed out. Otherwise it names the company and the date the clear starts from, and reminds you to run Repost Account Balances (R099102) in JD Edwards first. Cancel closes the dialog without changing anything. Go clears RapidReconciler’s copy of the GL from that date forward; it’s re-pulled on the next scheduled refresh.

Rebuild roll-forward re-merges the ledger RapidReconciler already holds and recomputes this report straight away. A status line under its description reports progress:

  • Running… while it works. The button reads Rebuilding… and can’t be clicked again.
  • Done — the ledger was re-merged and this report rebuilt. Reloading… when it finishes. The grid then reloads with the new figures.
  • A message naming what is holding the database, if the rebuild can’t start because something else is running (a scheduled refresh, for example). Try again once that finishes.
  • Finished, but the last step was … if the rebuild stopped with an error.
  • A message that the page lost contact with the data service. The rebuild keeps running anyway; reopen the page in a few minutes to see whether it finished.

Band 2: Audit Report

The grid lists every period for every account of the company, sorted by account and then period.

  • Account: narrows the grid to one account. All accounts shows them all.
  • Excel: downloads the rows on screen. The workbook names the database, company and account filter at the top, totals the amount columns, and shades break rows red and not-evaluated rows amber.
  • Row count: for example 17 rows of 68, the rows left after the account filter, out of all the company’s rows.
  • Click a column header to sort by it. Click again to reverse the sort.

Reading the columns

The amount columns show a dash for zero and parentheses for a negative.

ColumnWhat it means
CoThe company number.
AccountThe inventory GL account.
PeriodThe period end date the row covers.
Beg GLThe GL balance at the start of the period.
Per GLGL activity posted during the period.
End GLThe GL balance at the end of the period: Beg GL plus Per GL plus Unp Btch.
Unp BtchUnposted GL batches. They’re included in End GL but haven’t updated the account balances yet.
GL OKWhether the GL rolled forward from the period before. See GL OK and Var OK.
PerpThe perpetual inventory balance for the account at the end of the period.
Per CXNet cardex activity for the period.
Beg VarCarry forward: the variance the account brought in from earlier periods.
EODEnd of Day: cardex activity whose GL entries haven’t been created yet.
VarianceTransactions: the part of the variance that comes from this period’s transactions.
JEsManual entries: journal entries posted to the account with no cardex behind them.
OOBOut of balance: the gap between perpetual and the GL. It equals Beg Var + Variance + JEs + CX Var − Unp Btch − EOD.
CX VarCardex: item ledger discrepancies that none of the other columns account for.
Var OKWhether the variance rolled forward from the period before. See GL OK and Var OK.

GL OK and Var OK

Each row carries two chips, one for the GL and one for the variance.

ChipWhat it means
yesIt rolled forward cleanly from the period before.
noA break: it didn’t roll forward. These rows are the ones counted in the band and the AI brief.
baselineThe opening snapshot. Nothing comes before it, so both chips read baseline.
Not evaluatedThere’s no prior period to compare against, so the row was never checked. It isn’t a clean result.
end · yes, end · no, end · not evaluatedThe current open period, with its verdict. end · no counts as a break.
