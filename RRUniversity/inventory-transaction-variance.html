Transaction Variance

Transaction Details compares one document at a time: what the item ledger recorded against what the general ledger recorded, for the same document and the same account. This page explains what you are looking at and defines every column in the grid.

What the page compares

A transaction variance is a tie‑out. For a single document and account, the item‑ledger value and the ledger value either agree within tolerance or they do not. Variance is ledger − cardex, so a positive variance means the general ledger carries more value than the item ledger, and a negative one means the item ledger carries more.

It is not a percentage. The test is binary — the two sides tie or they do not — and a share of anything would mislead. Amounts are stated in dollars.

If the two sides do not tie, a correction is required. A card that explains why they disagree never means leave it alone; the cause decides what the correction is.

Variance Analyzer

The analyzer takes one question: for the transactions in view, does the account the cardex model says the value should reach match the account the transaction’s inventory DMAAI actually sent it to?

A green band means nothing was found. Every DMAAI in view resolves to the account the cardex model expects, so account routing is not the cause of anything on this screen. Look elsewhere — at cost, at timing, or at whether the entry was written at all.
An amber band names a count. It reads as “n of N DMAAIs off the cardex model”. Open it to see which routings disagree and what each posted to. A routing that disagrees is a configuration fix at the source, not a journal entry.

Findings

The Findings box is drafted from the analysis for the card you drilled in from. It is a draft: edit it if you would say it differently, then Save, and it travels with the card for hand‑off. It is not filed until you save it.

Not answered by this card

When it appears, this panel names rows that sit on an order whose other rows landed on a different card, and what they are worth.

The classifier claims one row at a time and stops at the first claim that matches. It never looks across an order. So a row can be claimed perfectly well while the rest of its order sits somewhere else — and working this card to completion would still leave the rest of that order open. The panel exists to say so.

These are disclosures, not verdicts. Nothing in the panel re‑classifies a row or contradicts the card. When the rows in view carry no disclosure, the panel does not render at all — a box reading “0 disclosures” on every screen is something you would learn to skim past.

Finding rows in a large view

  • Click Details to show or hide the grid. It starts closed, and once you open it the page remembers that in this browser.
  • Click any column header to sort; click again to reverse.
  • Signal sorts by gross with the largest first, because disclosed rows are a small share of any view and sorting them to the bottom would hide them.
  • The search box filters the rows in view. Prefix a term with ^ to match from the start of a value.
  • Excel exports what you are looking at, with the columns you have chosen, in the order you have them.
  • When a card or a filter narrows the grid, a Filtered pill appears in the grid header. It names what is narrowed and how many rows are left, for example Filtered: Co 90001 · unworked · 12 rows. Click it to clear what it names.
  • Worked: All filters by review status and owner. Under Worked status pick All transactions, Just worked or Not worked yet; under Owner pick Anyone or no one, Assigned to me or Unassigned. The pill then shows your choice, for example Worked: No · Mine.
  • The grid draws the first 500 rows. When there are more, the last line says so; narrow the filter or search to reach the rest. The totals still cover every row.

The icons at the start of each row

  • Analyze (the magnifier) opens a preview of the analysis for that one row. The heading names the document and order, with the variance, cardex and ledger amounts under it and a GL EXCESS or CARDEX EXCESS figure. The body has three parts: WHAT happened (the cardex total, the GL total, the account and period, and the variance with its pattern), WHY it happened and HOW to fix it. Export full Transaction Detail workbook › closes the preview and runs the row’s export. Close it with the ×, the Esc key, or a click outside it.
  • Export (the download arrow) builds the full Transaction Detail for that document, runs it through the Export Analyzer, and downloads a workbook with a Transaction Details tab and an Analysis tab. If the analyzer can’t finish, you get the workbook without the Analysis tab and a message saying so.

Updating several rows at once

Click a row to select it. Ctrl-click adds or removes a row, and Shift-click selects a range. As soon as one row is selected, an Update (N) button appears beside Worked: All, counting the rows you selected. It opens Update selected rows:

  • Worked: Worked or Not worked. When every selected row is in the same state, that one is already picked; when they differ, neither is.
  • Note: Leave unchanged keeps each row’s note. Replace with: opens the text box, and what you type replaces the note on every selected row.
  • Assign to: Leave unchanged, Unassigned, or a person. See Giving a row an owner.
  • Cancel closes without saving. Apply saves the changes to the selected rows.

Giving a row an owner

Any row can be handed to one person on your team. Owners help a team split up a period's exceptions and see who is working on what.

  • Select the rows, click Update (N), and choose a name under Assign to. Unassigned clears the owner. Assigning leaves Worked and the note alone unless you change those too.
  • The list shows the people who have access to this database and hold the analyst view (an Analyst or a Power User), because they are the ones who can open this page and work the row. Ask your RapidReconciler administrator if someone you expect is missing.
  • The Owner column shows the name. Hover over it to see the date the row was handed over. A worked row keeps its owner, shown dimmed, so you can still see who closed it.
  • The Worked filter has an Owner section. Assigned to me together with Not worked yet gives you your own open rows.
